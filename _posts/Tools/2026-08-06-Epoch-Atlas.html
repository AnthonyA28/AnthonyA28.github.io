    Data & method

    What this atlas shows—and what it cannot

    Historical territory is an interpretation, not a measurement. Boundaries may be approximate, overlapping, disputed, or inappropriate for societies that did not organize space as modern states do.

    Community boundary data

    The map uses locally hosted GeoJSON snapshots from André Ourednik’s Historical Basemaps project. The source is a work in progress, is provided without warranty, and asks users to verify it for academic work. A bundled copy is distributed under the GNU GPL 3.0.

    Connecting to the community dataset…

    Discrete snapshots, not invented annual borders

    The slider moves through dates that actually exist in the source. When you enter another year, Epoch Atlas chooses the nearest available snapshot and says which date is displayed. It does not interpolate boundaries between maps.

    Inspired by GeaCron, built from separate data

    GeaCron inspired the time-travel atlas concept, but its database is proprietary. No GeaCron map data, interface assets, or code are included here.

    Reading the map

    Colors group either related authorities or individual territories. Dashed and softened outlines represent lower values in the source’s BORDERPRECISION field. Pan with a mouse or one finger; zoom with the wheel, pinch gesture, or map buttons.
